---
title: "Cloudflare enforcement"
description: "Push the IPs your rules flag straight into your own Cloudflare account, so bots are blocked at the edge before they ever reach your origin."
---

Scoring tells you *which* visitors are automated. Enforcement acts on it. Connect your Cloudflare account and Botect keeps two IP lists in sync with the IPs your active rules flag; two WAF custom rules in your zone match against those lists and block or challenge at the edge.

**Botect is never in the request path.** Your traffic goes to Cloudflare, not to us. If Botect were unreachable, the lists already on Cloudflare keep enforcing exactly as they were at the last sync.

## Three things, three names

"Blocked" can mean three different things here, so Botect names them apart — in the dashboard and in this documentation:

| Name | What it is | Where you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blocklist** | The IPs Botect maintains in your Cloudflare account — one list to block, one to challenge | The **Blocklist** page |
| **Blocked requests** | Traffic Cloudflare actually stopped | The Blocklist page, read back from Cloudflare's analytics — Botect never observes these itself |
| **Verdicts** | Per-request decisions Botect returned to *your* server via the [verdict API](/endpoints/verdict) | The **Verdicts** page |

The distinction that catches people out: the blocklist is a *policy*, not a record of events. An IP can sit on it for a week without sending a single request.

Because Botect is deliberately not in the request path, it never witnesses a block. The per-IP counts on the Blocklist page are **read back from Cloudflare's analytics**, which is why they need the optional Zone Analytics permission and cover a rolling 24-hour window rather than all time.

<Callout kind="info">
  If you enforce at the edge and never call the verdict API, your Verdicts page stays empty. That is correct, not a fault: blocked traffic never reaches Botect to be decided on.
</Callout>

## The three stages

Enforcement is a chain, and every link has to hold. The Integrations page in your project mirrors these three stages, each with its own live status.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connection" icon="key" title-type="p">
    A scoped API token you create in Cloudflare, stored encrypted. It lets Botect maintain the IP lists and (optionally) the WAF rules.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Blocklist sync" icon="refresh-cw" title-type="p">
    Botect derives the blocklist from your active rules and pushes it into two IP lists in your Cloudflare account, on a cadence you choose.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Edge enforcement" icon="shield" title-type="p">
    Two WAF custom rules in your zone reference those lists. Botect can create and maintain them, or you can paste them in yourself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Creating the API token

In the Cloudflare account that owns the zone you're protecting, go to **My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token → Custom token** and add two policies:

| Scope | Permission | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specified domains — *your zone* | **Zone WAF Rules: Edit** | Create, verify, and repair the two custom rules |
| Specified domains — *your zone* | **Zone: Read** | List your zones for the zone picker |
| Specified domains — *your zone* | **Zone Analytics: Read** | Read back how many requests each listed IP had stopped |
| Entire account | **Account Rule Lists: Edit** | Maintain the two IP lists |

<Callout kind="tip">
  Zone Analytics is optional — everything enforces without it. Leave it out and the Blocklist page simply shows no per-IP counts. You can add it later by <strong>editing</strong> the existing token; the secret does not change, so nothing needs reconnecting.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="info">
  Cloudflare's newer dashboard labels these differently from the API. <strong>Account Filter Lists</strong> appears as <strong>Account Rule Lists</strong>, and it only shows up under an <strong>Entire Account</strong> policy — IP lists live on the account, not on a zone. <code>Edit</code> already implies read, so no separate read permission is needed.
</Callout>

Restrict the zone policy to the specific zone you're protecting rather than *All zones*, and prefer a non-expiring token — a token that expires silently stops enforcement from being updated.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Protect the zone that serves your <strong>site</strong>. If you run Botect-monitored traffic on one domain and have look-alike domains parked in the same account, only the zone actually serving requests needs the rules.
</Callout>

A **list-only** token (Account Rule Lists: Edit alone) also works. Botect will keep your lists in sync, and you create the two WAF rules yourself from the Manual tab.

## How the blocklist is derived

The list is not "every bot we've ever seen." It is computed from your project's **active** rules, and only from rules that can be honored at the edge:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Edge-enforceable rules only" icon="filter" title-type="p">
    An IP list is request-blind — it knows nothing about path, user agent, or country. So only rules whose expression references the <code>band</code> field <strong>exclusively</strong> are used. A rule like <code>band == "definite" AND path == "/login"</code> still works for live verdicts, but cannot be expressed as an IP list.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Aggregate by IP" icon="layers" title-type="p">
    Sessions in the rolling window are grouped per IP, excluding verified bots entirely.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Threshold and classify" icon="gauge" title-type="p">
    IPs with enough flagged sessions are assigned to the block or challenge list by the worst matching action.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Human-safety guard" icon="user-check" title-type="p">
    Any IP that has shown a <strong>genuine human</strong> in the window is downgraded to challenge — never hard-blocked. Shared IPs and carrier NAT are the reason this exists.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="alert">
  If your rules are all inactive, the blocklist is <strong>empty</strong> and a successful sync pushes zero IPs. Enforcement follows your active rules — activate one on the Rules page before expecting anything at the edge.
</Callout>

Verified bots are never listed, at any stage. See [verified bots](/verified-bots) for how that allowlist works.

## Syncing

Botect pushes deltas — added and removed IPs — rather than rewriting the lists. Pick a cadence in **Scoring settings → Enforcement** (from every 5 minutes up to weekly, or off for manual-only), or hit **Sync now** at any time.

Every sync also reconciles drift: it re-reads what is actually in Cloudflare and corrects out-of-band edits, so a list someone emptied by hand repopulates on the next run.

Cloudflare caps custom-list items account-wide (10,000 on non-Enterprise plans). If your blocklist exceeds the cap, the sync status reads **capped** and block entries are kept first — the highest-confidence IPs, ordered deterministically so the retained subset is stable between syncs rather than an arbitrary slice.

## The WAF rules

Two custom rules do the enforcing, and they work on every Cloudflare plan (they use `starts_with`, not the Business-and-above `matches` operator):

```text
Block rule      → action: Block
(ip.src in $botect_block_p1)

Challenge rule  → action: Managed Challenge
(ip.src in $botect_challenge_p1)
```

Botect appends them **after** any custom rules you already have, so your own rules keep their precedence.

### Managed vs manual

On the **Automatic** tab, pick your zone and Botect creates both rules, then verifies them on every sync:

- **Drifted** — you edited a managed rule in Cloudflare. Botect freezes it and stops touching it until you resync, which overwrites your change. The Resync dialog shows an expected-vs-actual diff first.
- **Missing** — the rule was deleted in Cloudflare. It is recreated automatically.

On the **Manual** tab, Botect gives you the exact expressions to paste and never touches your rules. Switch to manual whenever you need custom behavior — the existing rules stay in place, Botect just stops verifying them.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Don't edit managed rules directly in Cloudflare. Either let Botect own them, or switch to manual and own them yourself — editing a managed rule just freezes it until the next resync.
</Callout>

### Turning enforcement off

Disabling **Block** in Scoring settings flips that WAF rule's enabled switch off rather than deleting it, and the challenge rule folds the block list into its own expression — so those IPs are challenged instead of dropped. API paths are exempt by default; toggle that off to enforce on every path.

## Disconnecting

By default, disconnecting only stops the syncing: your WAF rules and IP lists stay exactly as they are, still enforcing, and Botect keeps their ids so a reconnection re-adopts them instead of creating duplicates. Botect is never the reason your protection disappears.

Two independent opt-ins let you clean up instead:

| Tick | What is removed | What survives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *(nothing)* | Nothing | Rules and lists keep enforcing at the edge |
| **Also remove the WAF rules Botect created** | The two custom rules | The lists, with the IPs they hold |
| **…and Also delete the IP lists** | The rules, then the lists | Nothing — the account is left clean |

The list option unlocks only once the rule option is ticked: Cloudflare refuses to delete a list while a rule still references it, so the rules have to go first. Each option is disabled when there is nothing for it to act on — a project that never created rules, or whose lists are already gone — with the reason shown beneath it.

Both are best-effort and never block the disconnect. Anything that could not be deleted is named in the result rather than reported as success, and Botect only forgets a rule or list it actually deleted, so whatever survives stays adoptable on reconnect.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Deleting the lists is the option to reach for when another environment will connect to the same Cloudflare account: leftover lists keep their names, and the next connection collides with them. See [Cloudflare list already exists](/troubleshooting/cloudflare-list-conflict).
</Callout>

## Self-hosting the list

If you enforce somewhere other than Cloudflare, the blocklist is available over the API and you can mirror it into any WAF, CDN, or firewall:

```bash
curl https://www.botect.ai/api/v1/blocklist \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_YOUR_PRIVATE_KEY"
```

It is [private-key authed](/authentication) and returns the same block and challenge sets the Cloudflare sync pushes.
